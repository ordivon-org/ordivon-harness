## 2. Post-Campaign-1 dependency delta

Campaign 1 changed the frontier in a material way.

Its final criterion depends on a weaker but still unresolved statement:

> Context may change while Run identity remains continuous, provided the relevant operational commitments and provenance are preserved.

Campaign 1 also established that byte/snapshot equality is neither necessary nor sufficient for Run identity, but deliberately stopped before defining general Context Equivalence.

Therefore P-B1 is no longer merely one peer open problem. It is now a **declared unresolved relation used by a completed derived theory**.

This creates the following post-Campaign dependency graph:

`Context Equivalence / Sufficiency`

→ conditions provider/model migration and reconstruction claims from Campaign 1  
→ supplies a criterion needed by Boundary Reconfiguration Equivalence  
→ constrains what context can cross Multi-Agent/federated handoff without silent semantic change  
→ constrains evidence/context sufficiency for Operational Accountability  
→ gives a cleaner test of cross-implementation invariance above concrete WorkingSet/WorkingView representations.

Rich Effect Semantics remains important but is more parallel than prerequisite: Campaign 1 proved that recovery can consume typed external effect standing without first solving a general effect ontology.

## 3. Typed candidate comparison

### C1 — Context Equivalence / Sufficiency

**Information gain:** very high after Campaign 1. The project now explicitly relies on provenance-preserving Context change but lacks a criterion for equivalence/sufficiency.

**Destructive-testability:** very high. Candidate contexts can vary by omission, addition, ordering, representation, generation, provenance, authority, stale/superseded status, transient-vs-durable placement, and model-visible projection.

**Architecture-falsification power:** high. Failure can revise ContextFrame, WorkingSet/WorkingView interpretation, provenance sufficiency, decision-sufficient-view claims, and Campaign-1 migration/reconstruction assumptions.

**Engineering evidence:** very high. Current Harness already exercises selected-vs-materialized context, WorkingSet transitions, WorkingView projection, caller ingress, transient Tool evidence, promotion, historical recall, stale/superseded generations, and source/model-view digest separation.

**Boundary-contamination risk:** comparatively low if equivalence is explicitly relative to a bounded TaskEpisode/decision problem and does not claim external truth equivalence.

**Dependency standing:** prerequisite/conditioning relation for several remaining candidates.

### C2 — Rich Effect Semantics

**Information gain:** high.

**Destructive-testability:** very high through delayed, duplicated, partial, irreversible, compensating and interfering effects.

**Architecture-falsification power:** high for Programme C.

**Engineering evidence:** high around Tool intent/fence/receipt/reconciliation.

**Boundary-contamination risk:** highest among near-term candidates. Physical effect truth remains Runtime/external-owner truth; causal/domain consequence belongs to World/domain owners; normative remedy/permission may belong to Normative.

**Dependency standing:** substantially independent of Context Equivalence. Campaign 1 showed typed effect standing can be consumed without a complete effect theory.

Decision: remains a strong parallel future campaign, but does not dominate the newly exposed Context bottleneck.

### C3 — Operational Accountability Graph

**Information gain:** high now that Run/Invocation identity is sharper.

**Destructive-testability:** moderate/high through missing evidence edges, dependency/common-cause errors, misattribution and contestability after change.

**Engineering evidence:** high via Run Receipt, CompletionProposal, Journal/CAS, Invocation/Tool evidence.

**Dependency standing:** no longer blocked by Run identity, but still consumes unresolved notions of evidence/context sufficiency. A graph can be mechanically built now, but its minimal sufficient contents cannot be stated cleanly before P-B1 is sharper.

Decision: likely downstream consumer of Campaign 2.

### C4 — Boundary Reconfiguration Equivalence

**Information gain:** high.

**Destructive-testability:** strong through internalization/externalization, capability placement changes and migration.

**Cross-owner significance:** very high with Runtime/Network/Computing.

**Dependency standing:** requires deciding which Context/reference/exposure changes preserve operational meaning and which constitute a changed cut. Context Equivalence is therefore a substantial prerequisite/conditioning relation.

Decision: defer until Context Equivalence gives a sharper invariant vocabulary.

### C5 — Multi-Agent / Federated Operational Identity

**Information gain:** potentially very high.

**Destructive-testability:** high in principle through handoff, concurrent authority, split-brain and cross-locus continuation.

**Boundary-contamination risk:** high; simultaneously touches Network, Host, Normative, Context and identity boundaries.

**Dependency standing:** Campaign 1 removed the single-Run identity prerequisite, but Context handoff sufficiency and boundary reconfiguration remain unresolved. Starting now would multiply two unresolved relations rather than isolate one.

Decision: defer.

### C6 — Cross-implementation invariance of Campaign-1 criterion

**Information gain:** important as falsification pressure.

**Destructive-testability:** currently limited by evidence source diversity. The current repository provides many mechanisms but one mature implementation family.

**Architecture-falsification power:** high if independent implementations/non-LLM controllers are available.

**Dependency standing:** better treated as a **mandatory falsification dimension** carried into future campaigns rather than a standalone campaign with insufficient independent evidence today.

Decision: do not select as standalone Campaign 2.

## 4. Dominance and prerequisite findings

Post-Campaign 1, Context Equivalence/Sufficiency has the strongest combination of:

- unresolved dependency used by an already-supported theory;
- high destructive-testability;
- mature current dogfood surfaces;
- relatively low owner-annexation risk;
- downstream leverage across Programme A, D and E plus future Multi-Agent work.

It does not dominate Rich Effect Semantics by universal importance. The result is narrower:

> **Context Equivalence is the current project bottleneck; Rich Effect Semantics remains an independent high-value parallel frontier.**

Cross-implementation invariance is not discarded; it becomes a campaign-level falsification requirement wherever feasible.

## 5. Tournament decision

**SELECTED CAMPAIGN 2:**

# Operational Context Equivalence
## Decision-Sufficient Views, Provenance & Safe Omission

Programme home: **B — Operational Epistemics**.

### Bounded campaign purpose

Derive and destructively test a technology-neutral, task-relative criterion for when two different operational Context states/views are equivalent or sufficiently interchangeable for a bounded Harness TaskEpisode/decision frontier, and when omission, substitution, provenance, authority or staleness differences invalidate that relation.

### Explicit non-goals

Campaign 2 will not:

- define external/world truth equivalence;
- equate identical model outputs with Context equivalence;
- solve general semantic equivalence of arbitrary text/data;
- make WorkingSet/WorkingView schemas the ontology;
- solve full Rich Effect Semantics;
- solve Boundary Reconfiguration or Multi-Agent mediation;
- build the final Accountability Graph;
- create HaF62.

## 6. Required destructive dimensions for Campaign 2

Campaign 2 must prebind fixtures covering at least:

- same bytes, different provenance/authority;
- different bytes, same bounded operational commitments;
- omission of irrelevant material;
- omission of decision-essential material;
- stale/superseded evidence versus current evidence;
- durable selected Context versus transient observation/history;
- representation/order/compression differences;
- source identity/generation substitution;
- model/provider-visible projection differences without silently redefining task semantics;
- forged or ungrounded Context expansion.

The evaluator must distinguish at least **equivalence**, **sufficiency**, and **validity** rather than forcing one boolean relation.

## 7. Cross-implementation falsification rule

Campaign 2 must state its criterion without relying on Python/SQLite/current class identities. Current WorkingSet/WorkingView mechanisms may dogfood it. Where possible, fixtures should compare multiple internal representation paths; however lack of an independent second Harness implementation must remain an explicit evidence limit rather than being hidden.
